> “The minute you think that you’ve got it made, you’re perfect, or that you’ve arrived, you’re dead in the water.” 
> — David Goggins.

When you spend a long enough time walking through a desert, finding an oasis is a lifesaver and feels like a victory. You drop your bags, sit down and finally rest, never wanting to leave. It is human nature to get tricked into believing that you’ve ‘made it’. You forget that an oasis only offers survival, not a full life, and that it was only ever supposed to be a checkpoint, not the final destination. 

The corporate world operates in the exact same way. 

The greatest threat to a company that has just survived a crisis is the illusion of safety, and Nvidia knew that. They didn’t tread water; they set out to conquer the ocean. For them, mere survival was never the end game. 

They wanted to command the tide. 

So in 1999 they released their fifth chip, the GeForce 256, creating an entirely new category of computing hardware and calling it the world’s first Graphics Processing Unit (GPU). This chip didn’t just accelerate pixels; rather it was fully programmable. Nvidia’s GPUs were highly specialised computational units that were designed to do only one specific type of heavy task, but with incredible efficiency. Unlike ordinary graphics cards at the time, that waited for instructions from the main processor, GPUs were autonomous computational machines that were specifically built to take over the heavy graphical workloads and execute them with blinding speed.

The impact of these GPUs were immediate and significant.

PC gaming suddenly exploded in creativity and visual fidelity. For the first time, developers were no longer bottlenecked by the processing limits of a computer’s central processor. They could build massive, immersive 3D worlds with complex textures, dynamic lighting, different environments and much more. This changed the gaming industry forever, and Nvidia went public that very year. 

Their GPUs were the new foundational standard for high performance technology and their hardware was the only logical choice for the biggest players in the market. 

In a perfect twist of irony, Microsoft - the exact company whose DirectX software nearly drove Nvidia into the ground - had to use GeForce to power its new project: The original Xbox console, turning Microsoft into their biggest customer. 

This was a monumental victory, yet it did not cure Jensen’s Paranoia. He looked at the PC market and saw a terrifying ceiling, the overall growth was beginning to plateau. 

> “Always assume you are about to be replaced” 
> — Bill Gates 

Jensen realised that by remaining a PC component manufacturer, they would eventually be cornered into a brutal margin crushing war with established giants like Intel and AMD, or worse, they could be entirely replaced by them. To achieve absolute invincibility, the didn’t just need a new chip. They needed to invent an entirely new, zero-billion dollar market. 

They were about to realise that the gpu they built was capable of calculating far more than just pixels.
